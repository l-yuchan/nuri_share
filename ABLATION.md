# fastText initialization scale and word alignment

This experiment isolates two suspected causes of the earlier pretrained
classifier's lower score: vector magnitude and the association between words
and pretrained vectors. It reuses the exact saved CBOW word vectors; no corpus
model is retrained and no validation labels guide vector construction.

## Reproduction

Use the project's Python 3.12 semantic environment:

```sh
export UV_PROJECT_ENVIRONMENT=.venv-semantic
uv sync --locked --extra semantic
uv run --extra semantic python ablation_experiment.py
uv run --extra semantic python analyze_ablation.py
```

On PowerShell, replace the export with
`$env:UV_PROJECT_ENVIRONMENT = ".venv-semantic"`.
The prerequisite is `artifacts/fasttext/nuri_train.vec`, generated by the
earlier `fasttext_experiment.py` run. Its hash is recorded in the new metadata.
Rebuilding CBOW with multiple threads can produce different source vectors,
so use the saved vectors to reproduce this particular comparison.

## Fixed design

- The original 666 training / 158 validation rows, with 13 validation groups.
- Five paired seeds: 13, 42, 73, 101, 137; 25 classifier fits in total.
- Every classifier: 100 epochs, learning rate 0.5, 100 dimensions, word bigrams,
  character subwords of length 2-5, 200,000 buckets and one training thread.
- Identical normalized training text, labels and word IDs in every condition.
  Each run checks its dictionary against the epoch-zero native dictionary.
- Only training-vocabulary words plus EOS are exported. Validation-only words
  do not enter the initialization file.

The conditions are native `scratch`, `aligned_raw`, `aligned_scaled`,
`shuffled_raw` and `shuffled_scaled`.

For each seed, an epoch-zero native classifier supplies the median raw input
word-row norm, excluding EOS: approximately 0.0576. The source median is
8.5432. Scaling multiplies **every vector by one common positive factor**,
approximately 0.00675. This preserves directions, cosine similarities and
relative lengths; it is not individual unit normalization. Calibration uses
raw word rows, not the final word-plus-subword representation.

Shuffling constructs a seeded random single cycle over all ordinary words.
Every ordinary word receives another word's vector; no ordinary word remains
fixed. EOS retains its own vector within each magnitude condition. The vector
multiset and its norm distribution are preserved. The same shuffled mapping
is used for raw and scaled conditions within a seed. This samples single-cycle
permutations, not uniformly from all possible derangements.

Word permutation changes the association of words with both vector directions
and vector lengths. Thus it tests the usefulness of lexical alignment as a
whole, rather than proving a purely semantic effect independent of word norms.
Both pretrained treatments still transfer only word vectors, not the CBOW
subword table.

## Results

Values are means across five seeds. The standard deviation describes seed
variation on this fixed validation set, not uncertainty over new datasets.

| Initialization | Accuracy | Seed SD, percentage points | Macro-F1 | Train accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Native scratch | 62.03% | 0.63 | 0.6079 | 100% |
| Aligned, original scale | 55.82% | 0.28 | 0.5513 | 100% |
| Aligned, corrected scale | 60.00% | 0.28 | 0.5881 | 100% |
| Shuffled, original scale | 52.66% | 3.21 | 0.5199 | 100% |
| Shuffled, corrected scale | 60.00% | 0.28 | 0.5882 | 100% |

Seed 42 reproduces the earlier scratch and original-scale pretrained scores
exactly: 62.66% / 0.6148 and 55.70% / 0.5500 respectively.

The following intervals use 5,000 paired bootstrap replicates. Each replicate
resamples whole validation groups and training seeds, using the same samples
for all conditions. Rows within a selected group stay together; scores pool
their rows before averaging per-seed scores. Macro-F1 retains all five labels
even if a bootstrap sample omits a label.

| Contrast, treatment minus control | Accuracy change, pp | Exploratory 95% interval, pp | Macro-F1 change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Aligned scaled - aligned raw | +4.18 | [-7.64, +15.95] | +0.0369 |
| Shuffled scaled - shuffled raw | +7.34 | [-4.31, +19.49] | +0.0683 |
| Aligned scaled - shuffled scaled | 0.00 | [-1.54, +1.28] | -0.00006 |
| Aligned raw - shuffled raw | +3.16 | [-3.33, +10.21] | +0.0314 |
| Aligned scaled - scratch | -2.03 | [-5.34, +1.22] | -0.0197 |

All five seeds improve when aligned vectors are rescaled. After rescaling,
aligned and shuffled conditions have the same number of correct validation
predictions for each seed. Their class-wise outcomes differ slightly; equal
accuracy does not mean identical predictions.

## Interpretation for development

1. On this fixed validation set, changing magnitude alone recovers 4.18
   accuracy points on average. The scale mismatch is a supported contributor
   under the tested training settings.
2. After correction, correct word-vector associations provide no observed
   aggregate advantage over shuffled associations. The current transfer method
   has not demonstrated a benefit from lexical alignment at the corrected
   scale. This does not prove that the vectors contain no semantic information:
   training could overwrite it, or its task-relevant effect could be small.
3. Correction alone does not beat native scratch. It should be treated as an
   initialization control, rather than adopted as a demonstrated improvement.
   The original-scale aligned condition does outperform shuffled vectors on
   average, so the evidence also does not justify claiming that alignment is
   universally useless.
4. All classifiers fit every training example. The remaining issue is
   generalization under this representation and transfer method, not inability
   to fit the training labels.

For subsequent development, keep magnitude calibration and shuffled controls
when testing richer transfer methods. Transfer of learned subword features,
domain-relevant text and Korean tokenization are reasonable separate next
experiments; they were not tested here. Keep the existing TF-IDF model as the
current performance baseline.

These are post-hoc exploratory ablations on an already inspected validation
set. The five training seeds do not create five independent validation sets.
There are only 13 validation groups, and every reported group-aware interval
includes zero. The observed improvements therefore do not establish a
population-wide gain, equivalence between treatments, or an independent test
score. Intervals are percentile estimates without multiplicity adjustment,
conditional on the saved corpus vectors and supplied split.
